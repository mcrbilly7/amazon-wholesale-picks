# Ads plan for a Texas Amazon reseller with $500 total capital

**Research date:** September 23, 2026 (CT)  
**Scope:** Amazon Sponsored Products, Meta Ads, and Google Ads. This is a budget and execution plan, not a promise of sales or a legal/tax opinion.

## Executive recommendation

**Do not spend on paid ads before the first inventory purchase.** With only $500, inventory, inbound shipping/prep, Amazon fees, returns, and a cash buffer have priority. An ad click cannot fix a listing that is not buyable, in stock, Featured Offer (Buy Box) eligible, competitively priced, or profitable after fees. Sponsored Products also generally require an active Professional selling account, an eligible new product/category, the ability to ship throughout the marketplace, and Featured Offer eligibility. If there is no stocked, eligible ASIN, Amazon Sponsored Products cannot be a useful live test.

After buying a small, validated first lot, verify landed cost, Amazon fees, contribution margin, listing quality, inventory cover, and Featured Offer rate. Then test **Amazon Sponsored Products first**: it reaches shoppers already searching on Amazon and uses the existing product detail page, whereas Meta and Google usually require off-Amazon creative, a destination/landing page, tracking, and more audience/measurement work. Keep enough cash to reorder and handle returns; do not treat the whole $500 as an ad budget.

A practical sequence is to make the first ad test a small, time-boxed experiment (for example, $30-$50 total only after inventory is live), and scale only if the observed conversion rate and ad cost still leave positive contribution margin. These are guardrails, not pricing or performance guarantees.

## If the seller insists on advertising now

First determine whether “now” means an already-stocked, buyable ASIN. If not, do not launch: prepare the campaign and creatives, but wait. If an eligible ASIN is already in stock, use **one channel only** and cap the experiment:

1. **Preferred test — Amazon Sponsored Products:** one ASIN, one automatic campaign, conservative bid, and a 3-day end date. Try **$5/day** only if the account accepts it; if the US account enforces the newer $10 marketplace setting, use **$10/day for 3 days** instead. Planned exposure is $15-$30, but monitor each day and pause if spend or economics are poor. Amazon’s published FAQ says the technical minimum is $1/day, while its newer US getting-started guidance and best-practice guidance use/recommend $10; the account UI and campaign type control what can actually be entered. Amazon daily budgets are not necessarily an intraday hard stop, so an end date and active monitoring matter.
2. **Meta only if there is a real destination and a reason to build demand:** use one ad set and a **$25-$35 lifetime budget over 5-7 days** rather than several daily-budget ad sets. A lifetime budget gives a clearer total ceiling. Meta has no single universal minimum; it varies by country, objective, currency, buying type, bid strategy, and schedule. Daily budgets are averages and can spend more on an individual day, so do not assume `$5/day` is a hard cap.
3. **Google only if there is a compliant website/landing page and conversion measurement:** use one tightly scoped Search campaign with a short **$20-$35 total test** (or the lowest accepted average daily budget with a fixed end date and monitoring). Google has no universal platform minimum, but most campaigns may spend up to 2x the average daily budget on a day and up to 30.4x it over a billing month. Google should not be the first test for a seller with no website, feed, or conversion tracking.

Do not run Amazon, Meta, and Google simultaneously with this capital. A small test is for learning (click/search-term, listing conversion, and unit economics), not a basis for a sales forecast.

## What must be prepared

### Amazon Sponsored Products

- A live, buyable ASIN with inventory, a competitive offer, Featured Offer eligibility, and a compliant category/listing.
- Seller Central Professional account, shipping coverage for the marketplace, and an accepted advertising payment method.
- The listing assets do most of the creative work: accurate title, compliant main image, useful secondary images, bullets, description/A+ content where available, price, and reviews that were earned legitimately.
- Campaign structure, starting bid, automatic or manual targets, negative keywords/products, start/end date, and a measurement sheet for spend, clicks, orders, conversion rate, TACoS/ACoS, and contribution margin.
- Sponsored Products generally needs **no custom ad creative**; it draws from the product detail page. This does not make a weak listing ready to advertise.

### Meta Ads

- Meta Business Portfolio, ad account, Facebook Page and/or Instagram account; a catalog/feed if using catalog ads.
- Two or three properly licensed product images and/or short vertical video, primary text, headline, CTA, destination URL, and a clear audience hypothesis.
- A website/landing page is preferable for control and tracking. If sending shoppers to Amazon, use an appropriate Amazon destination and understand that off-site conversion measurement is more limited; use Amazon Attribution where eligible and available.
- Pixel/dataset and events only when the seller controls a compliant website and has implemented them correctly. Do not install tracking code on Amazon.

### Google Ads

- Google Ads account, billing setup, keyword/negative-keyword list, responsive Search headlines/descriptions, and a compliant destination page.
- For Shopping or other feed-based campaigns, a properly configured Merchant Center feed and required business/product information; do not assume an Amazon detail-page URL qualifies as a full retail website or gives reliable conversion tracking.
- Conversion tags/analytics on a seller-controlled site, or another defensible measurement method. Without this, Google traffic is difficult to evaluate responsibly.

## Access, drafting, and approval boundaries

**Access needed for an actual build or launch:**

- **Seller Central:** the owner should invite a named user with only the advertising/campaign permissions needed, and retain primary account, finance, and security control. A live Sponsored Products campaign also needs the eligible listing and an advertising payment method.
- **Meta:** the owner should grant the needed ad-account advertiser/partial asset access in Business Suite. Meta’s Admin role can change payment methods and permissions; an Advertiser can create/edit ads but should not need billing control. Page, Instagram, catalog, pixel/dataset, and domain permissions may be separate.
- **Google:** grant the least-privilege Google Ads role needed (for example, Standard for campaign work or Read-only for reporting). Google Ads account access and the payments-profile/billing-user permission are separate. The owner should retain admin and payment control.

Never send passwords, MFA codes, or payment-card numbers. Use platform invitations and keep billing with the LLC owner.

**Can be drafted with no spend:** channel recommendation; campaign naming and structure; target/negative-keyword list; bid and budget guardrails; ad copy, headlines, and a creative brief; landing-page outline; UTM/Amazon Attribution plan; launch checklist; and a reporting template. A campaign can also be saved as a draft where the platform permits it, but account access is still required.

**Requires explicit owner approval and usually payment:** publishing/activating any campaign; adding or changing a card, billing profile, or spending limit; purchasing photography, video, software, a domain, or a landing page; granting account access; using manufacturer/brand assets where rights are not documented; and increasing a budget or extending an end date. No ads should be run or posted on the seller’s behalf without that approval.

## Compliance and identity notes

- The business may truthfully identify itself as a Texas LLC seller/reseller. An LLC formation does **not** by itself make the business an Amazon partner, authorized reseller, or brand representative.
- Do not say or imply “Amazon partner,” “Amazon-sponsored,” “endorsed by Amazon,” or an affiliation with Amazon unless the specific authorization exists. Do not use Amazon logos, Prime marks, or other Amazon branding beyond what the relevant platform policy permits.
- Do not claim “authorized reseller,” “official distributor,” “genuine,” “lowest price,” “guaranteed,” health/safety results, or other superiority/performance claims unless the seller can substantiate them and the claims comply with Amazon, Meta, Google, FTC, and applicable state/federal rules.
- Preserve wholesale invoices and supply-chain records; sell only authentic, policy-compliant products and do not alter packaging or manipulate reviews. Check category, brand, condition, and intellectual-property restrictions before purchasing inventory.
- Use the LLC’s truthful legal/business information in account and ad settings. Separately confirm Texas sales-tax registration/collection, marketplace-facilitator treatment, federal tax, and resale-certificate obligations with the Texas Comptroller or a qualified tax professional. This plan does not determine those obligations.
- Review each platform’s current ad, landing-page, trademark, privacy, and prohibited-products rules immediately before launch. Policies and budget interfaces change.
